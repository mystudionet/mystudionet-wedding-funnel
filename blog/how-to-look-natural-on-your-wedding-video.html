How to Look Natural on Your Wedding Video

By Kursad Yonet · October 7, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

The couples who look most natural on film are the ones who forget the camera is there. This comes down to the filmmaker's approach as much as anything you do. Mystudionet Productions uses a cinematic documentary style, filming real moments rather than directing poses, so you spend your wedding day focused on each other, not on a lens.

Almost everyone says some version of this to me before the wedding. "We are not photogenic." "We feel awkward in front of cameras." "How do we not look stiff?" After more than 20 years and 300+ weddings filmed across Suffolk County, Nassau County, the Hamptons and New Jersey, here is what I have learned: how you look on your wedding film has far less to do with you than you think.

Why do couples look stiff on wedding videos?

Usually it is because the filmmaker is directing them. The stilted-looking wedding videos you have seen, the ones where the couple stands in a garden staring at the camera while dramatic strings play, are staged. They are posed like photographs. When someone tells you where to stand, how to angle your shoulders, where to put your chin, the result looks exactly like what it is: a person following instructions in front of a camera.

My approach is the opposite. I film what is actually happening. I am not directing you; I am watching you. I stay close enough to catch the real moments and far enough back that you stop thinking about me.

Does a first look help you feel comfortable on camera?

It often does, but for reasons people do not expect. A first look is not primarily a photo opportunity. It is the one quiet moment on a day that will move very fast. You and your partner see each other before the ceremony, somewhere private, away from the crowd. The relief and emotion are real, and they happen whether the camera is there or not.

What I notice filming first looks is that once that moment passes, both partners are visibly looser for the rest of the day. You have already seen each other. The anticipation is gone. The camera becomes background noise. That ease carries through the portraits, the cocktail hour, the ceremony itself.

What should we actually do during portraits?

Talk to each other. Move. Do something. The worst thing you can do during a portrait session is stand still and wait to be told something looks good.

What works: say something real to your partner. Not a posed line, just something true. A memory from when you first met, what you are looking forward to about tonight, something you noticed about them during the first look. Your face will do something honest, and that is the frame I am after.

Walk somewhere together. Actually go somewhere, not pretend to walk while standing still. If you are at a venue on the North Shore or in the Hamptons, step toward something: the water, the treeline, the garden path. Movement gives me real moments to catch. Standing still gives me poses, and poses age badly.

What should we do during the ceremony?

Forget the camera completely. The ceremony is not a performance for the film. It is the most important few minutes of your day, and my job is to be invisible in it.

Look at your partner. Listen to what is being said. When you repeat your vows, say them to the person in front of you, not to the room. I will find the shots. You will not have to help me.

What kills a ceremony on film is when one partner keeps glancing toward the camera. The lens catches it every time. The moment you stop performing and actually feel what is happening, the film changes.

Does the length of coverage change how natural you look?

More time means less pressure on any single moment. With 8 hours of coverage, the moments are spread across a full day: getting ready in the morning, the ceremony, cocktail hour, first dance, toasts, dinner. By the time the reception is in full swing, you have been on camera for hours and it feels completely ordinary.

One of the reasons I built a longer option into my packages, up to 12 hours for bigger weddings, is that by hour four, people stop performing. That is when the real film starts. You cannot fake the ease that comes from time, and no amount of coaching before the wedding gets you there faster.

Frequently asked questions

Will we need to rehearse anything before the wedding?

No rehearsal is needed. Cinematic documentary filmmaking does not require any preparation from the couple. I arrive ready, scout the light and the spaces, and film what happens. The only thing I ask is that you let me know about any timing changes and point out important family members before the ceremony starts.

What if we are nervous the whole day?

Nerves are visible on camera, and they are also completely real. Some of the most moving wedding films I have made were with couples who were obviously nervous throughout. What matters is that you stay focused on each other rather than on performing for the film. The nerves fade naturally as the day moves forward.

Should we watch our film before we share it publicly?

Yes. Your film will be delivered through a private link so you can watch it together first before sharing it with family or posting it anywhere. Delivery takes 8 to 12 weeks. Most couples say that watching it the first time brings the whole day back in a way that surprised them.
