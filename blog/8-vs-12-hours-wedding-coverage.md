# 8 Hours vs 12 Hours of Wedding Coverage: What Each Actually Includes

By Kursad Yonet, Wedding Filmmaker at Mystudionet Productions
Published: October 28, 2026
Canonical: https://wedding.mystudionet.com/blog/8-vs-12-hours-wedding-coverage

## Quick answer

Mystudionet Productions offers 8-hour coverage starting at $3,500 and a 12-hour premium package for larger days. The 8-hour package covers getting ready through early reception dancing, including the ceremony, toasts and first dances. The 12-hour package adds extended getting-ready footage, full cocktail hour, late-night dancing and the grand exit. Which one fits depends on how many hours your celebration runs from start to finish.

This question comes up on almost every consultation call I take, usually once we have talked about style and the couple starts thinking practically. They want to know: is 8 hours enough, or do we need 12? The honest answer is that it depends on your day, not mine. Here is how I think through it with every couple I work with across Long Island.

## What does an 8-hour wedding film package actually cover?

Eight hours is the foundation of what I do at Mystudionet Productions, and for most Long Island weddings it is enough to tell the whole story. With 8 hours on your day, I can cover:

- Getting ready, usually one or both sides depending on how the schedule lines up
- First look or pre-ceremony portraits if you have one planned
- The full ceremony, from processional through recessional
- Cocktail hour highlights
- Grand entrance into the reception
- First dance
- Parent dances
- Toasts
- Cake cutting
- Early dancing

For most couples in Suffolk County and Nassau County on a standard timeline, that list is the whole story. The moments most couples want to revisit most in ten years are all inside those 8 hours. After 300+ weddings, I can say with confidence that 8 hours rarely leaves people feeling something was missed, as long as the timeline is built around it.

## What does the 12-hour package cover that 8 hours cannot?

The 12-hour premium package adds roughly four hours to work with, and those hours land in specific places. Early in the day, I have time with both sides getting ready without having to choose one over the other when schedules overlap. At the reception, I can stay well past dinner and catch the full dance floor, not just the first songs.

If you have a grand exit planned, a late-night sendoff, or a private last dance you want documented, the 12-hour package is built for that. On East End and Hamptons weddings, where receptions often run later and the venue grounds offer more room to move around during golden hour, the extra time makes a real difference in what I can bring back to the edit.

The other thing longer coverage buys you is flexibility. Weddings with two venues, large wedding parties where portraits take longer than expected, or ceremonies that run over time all benefit from not having to watch the clock. When I am not thinking about time, the filming feels more relaxed, and that shows in the footage.

## How do you decide which package fits your day?

I ask couples one simple question: what time does getting ready start, and what time does the reception end? If the total span is 9 hours or less, 8 hours of coverage is almost always enough. If the span is 11 hours or more, the 12-hour package is the right call.

In between those two points, it comes down to what you most want filmed. If you want me there for the grand exit and late dancing, that leans toward 12 hours. If you are fine with the film ending around the first hour of dancing, 8 hours is the more efficient choice and still gives you a complete, full story on film.

For context: on the North Shore and South Shore of Long Island, most of my couples choose 8 hours. On the East End and in the Hamptons, the split is closer to even because the days run longer. Larger celebrations and those with multiple ceremony locations nearly always go with 12.

## Does the package length change the quality of the final film?

No. The editing, color grading and licensed music are exactly the same in both packages. What changes is the scope of what the film can cover, not how it is made or how much care goes into it. A well-edited 8-hour film is not a lesser film. It is a tighter film, built from the core of your day.

Think of it this way: more hours gives me more material to find the quiet looks, the candid laughs and the small moments between moments. Quality is a function of who is behind the camera and how the footage is assembled, not how many hours the camera ran.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does 8 hours of coverage include the ceremony and reception?

For most Long Island weddings, yes. Getting ready, the ceremony, cocktail hour highlights, first dance, toasts and early reception dancing all fit inside 8 hours, provided the timeline runs roughly from noon to late evening. If your celebration runs 11 hours or more from start to finish, you may want to consider the 12-hour package.

### What time does filming typically start for an 8-hour package?

That depends on your specific timeline, not a fixed rule. I work backward from the ceremony. If you want me there for getting ready, we figure out how many hours before the ceremony that needs to start, and I arrive when the footage matters most. On a noon ceremony, I often arrive around 9 or 10 in the morning.

### Can I add more hours after I have already booked?

Yes, as long as I have the time available on your date. If your day grows after booking, reach out as early as possible. Coverage upgrades are easier to arrange with more notice, especially for peak season Saturdays in the Hamptons and on the East End.

### Are 8-hour and 12-hour highlight films the same length when delivered?

Generally yes. The highlight film is typically the same runtime regardless of the package. What changes is the range of moments I have to draw from. A 12-hour day gives me a larger well of footage to find the very best takes, not a longer finished film.
