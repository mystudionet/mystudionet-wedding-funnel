## What is a micro wedding or elopement film, exactly?

A micro wedding is typically any wedding with 20 or fewer guests, though the number is flexible. An elopement is usually just the couple and a witness or two, sometimes no witnesses at all beyond the officiant. The legal minimum is the same as any wedding; the visual and emotional story is just much more concentrated.

The film itself covers the same beats as a larger wedding: getting ready if that is happening, the ceremony, portraits together, and any dinner or gathering afterward. What changes is the pace and the access. Without a receiving line of 150 people or a cocktail hour spread across three rooms, the camera can stay close. I am not navigating crowds or running between locations. The edit tends to be tighter and more personal, because nearly every minute of the day made it into the footage.

## Does a small wedding mean a less impressive film?

In my experience, no. The films that have moved people most deeply are not always the ones with the grandest venues or the longest receptions. A ten-minute elopement film shot at a bluff on the North Shore in late afternoon light, with the couple's vows fully audible and no background noise from a DJ, can hold its own against anything I have filmed at a formal ballroom event. The story is what carries it, and two people getting married is a complete story regardless of who is watching.

What elopements and micro weddings often have that larger weddings struggle to achieve is stillness. There is room to hear what the officiant is actually saying. There is space for the couple to be themselves rather than performers at the front of a room. The camera notices the difference. I notice the difference in the edit. The resulting film is frequently more watchable ten years later than a highlights reel from a larger event where everyone was performing for the crowd.

My packages start at $3,500 for 8 hours of coverage. For most micro weddings, that is more than enough time. An elopement that runs two or three hours still uses the base package, because travel to the location, setup time, and the editing and color grading afterward take the same number of hours regardless of guest count. I am building the film around the two of you, the light at that location that day, and whatever exchanges happen in the quiet moments between the ceremony and the portraits. ## Where are good places to elope or hold a micro wedding on Long Island?

Long Island has real options in every direction. The East End and the Hamptons offer beaches and vineyard properties with strong natural light and minimal crowds outside peak summer weekends. The North Shore has bluffs above the Sound, historic estates and gardens that hold up beautifully on camera in every season. Nassau County and Suffolk County have parks, waterfront properties and smaller venues that work well for gatherings of 20 or fewer. The question is usually about season and time of day as much as location. I film across all of these areas regularly and can give specific advice on light and logistics once you tell me what you have in mind.

## What should we think about before booking a micro wedding film?

Three things matter most. First, tell me the ceremony location and approximate timing before the wedding day. Even for a two-hour elopement, knowing whether the ceremony happens at 10:00 a.m. or 4:30 p.m. changes everything about how I prepare for the light. Second, let me know if you want any particular exchange captured on audio, such as personal vows that are not in the standard ceremony script. That affects microphone placement. Third, if there is a dinner or gathering afterward that you want in the film, include it in the brief. I can usually stay for a few hours of that as well within the standard package.

Everything else I can figure out when I arrive. Smaller weddings are, in a certain way, easier to film well, because the story is not trying to be in three places at once. If you are planning something small and honest and you want a real film to show for it, that is exactly what I do.

### Can we have a wedding film if we elope with just two witnesses?

Yes, and it is often one of the most cinematic films to make. Without a large guest list to manage, the camera can stay close and the film can focus entirely on the two of you. Some of the most personal films I have made were elopements. The story is tighter and the emotion tends to come through more clearly when nothing else is competing for the frame.

### Where are good locations for a micro wedding or elopement on Long Island?

Long Island has strong options across all areas. The East End and the Hamptons offer beaches and vineyard settings with excellent natural light. The North Shore has bluffs, gardens and historic estates. Nassau County and Suffolk County have parks and waterfront properties that photograph beautifully year-round. The right location depends on the season, the look you want on film, and how many guests you are bringing.

### Do we still need a full wedding day timeline for a micro wedding film?

A simplified one, yes. Even a two-hour elopement benefits from knowing the ceremony start time, any planned portrait window, and whether there is a dinner or gathering afterward that you want captured. The timeline does not need to be complicated, but sharing it before the day lets me plan the shot list properly and make sure nothing important gets missed.
