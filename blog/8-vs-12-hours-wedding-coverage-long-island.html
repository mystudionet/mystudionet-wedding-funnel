8 Hours vs. 12 Hours of Wedding Coverage: What Each Package Actually Gets You

By Kursad Yonet · September 30, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Eight hours of wedding coverage typically runs from the final getting-ready moments through the first dances, capturing the core of the day. Twelve hours extends through late reception dancing, the send-off, and any golden-hour portrait time at your venue. Mystudionet Productions packages start at $3,500 for 8 hours, with a premium option up to 12 hours. Which you need depends on your venue timeline, your getting-ready location, and whether you want the full arc of the night in your film.

I get asked this question at nearly every consultation, usually right after the pricing question. And the honest answer is not "more is always better." It is "it depends on your day." After 20 years and 300+ weddings across Suffolk County, Nassau County, the Hamptons, and New Jersey, I can usually tell within five minutes of looking at a couple's timeline which package makes sense for them.

What does 8 hours of wedding coverage actually include?

For most Long Island weddings, 8 hours covers the core of the day beautifully. I typically arrive 45 minutes to an hour before the ceremony, which means I am with you for the final moments of getting ready: the dress going on, the veil being placed, the groom taking a breath in the mirror. From there we move into the ceremony, cocktail hour details, the first look portraits if they happen after the ceremony, the grand entrance, dinner, toasts, first dances, and cake cutting.

For a Saturday ceremony that starts at 5 PM, 8 hours takes you to roughly 11 PM, which covers the main events cleanly. For a Jewish ceremony at noon on Long Island's North Shore, 8 hours almost always runs you through the full dinner reception. The math works out more often than people expect.

Eight hours works especially well when:

  • The ceremony runs 30 to 45 minutes
  • The reception is in the same building or on the same grounds as the ceremony, common at North Shore country clubs and South Shore ballrooms
  • The couple is not doing a first look and the getting-ready location is close to the venue
  • The wedding is a smaller, tighter celebration where the schedule is intentionally compact

What does 12 hours of coverage add that 8 hours does not?

Four extra hours sounds modest. In practice, it changes what the final film can be.

With 12 hours I can arrive while people are still having breakfast and their hair is still in rollers. I can stay through the last guests on the dance floor, the final slow song, and the send-off whether that is a sparkler exit, a getaway car, or the couple's families saying goodbye on the steps. I can also shoot a second round of portraits during golden hour if the venue timeline allows it, which on a Hamptons or East End property can produce some of the best footage of the entire film.

There is something else that happens late in a reception that is almost impossible to capture on a shorter package. After 10 PM, when everyone has been dancing for two hours and the ties are loosened and nobody is performing for the camera anymore, that is when the realest moments happen. Old friends who flew in from out of town pulling the couple aside for one more hug. Parents standing at the edge of the floor watching their kid dance. Those moments are quiet and they go fast, and you only get them if the camera is still in the room.

Twelve hours works best when:

  • The getting-ready location is far from the venue, especially for Hamptons or East End weddings where driving time eats into the day
  • The couple wants a first look and separate portrait session, plus golden-hour portraits later
  • The reception is large and the energy genuinely peaks late in the night
  • The wedding includes multiple locations, such as a church ceremony followed by a separate venue in Nassau County or Suffolk County

Does more coverage time automatically mean a better film?

No. I want to be direct about this because it matters when you are making a budget decision.

More time in the room gives you more material and a longer arc of the day, but it does not by itself make the film feel more alive. What makes a film feel like something is the style, the editing, and the experience of the person holding the camera. A skilled documentary-style filmmaker can make 8 hours feel complete and emotionally whole. A less experienced one can have 12 hours of footage and still miss what matters.

The questions to ask yourself are practical ones: Does my timeline actually need more than 8 hours? Will the meaningful moments happen before or after the 8-hour mark? If the answer to both is yes, book 12 hours. If not, 8 is enough.

How do I figure out which package is right for my Long Island wedding?

When couples ask me this, I work through three questions with them.

Where are you getting ready, and how far is that from the venue? If you are getting ready at a hotel in Garden City and the ceremony is at a venue in the Hamptons, you are losing 90 minutes of potential coverage to driving. That changes the math considerably. In that case, 12 hours gives you room to capture both locations without cutting anything short.

Are you doing a first look? A first look before the ceremony condenses the portrait timeline and usually means the couple is more relaxed on camera for the rest of the day. It also creates a natural moment for a second set of portraits during golden hour, which is often the most beautiful light at any Long Island venue, whether you are on the water in the Hamptons or at an estate on the North Shore.

What time does your reception actually end? Not the venue's official closing time. What time does the energy peak, and what do you want captured after it? If you want the send-off in your film and your send-off happens at midnight, plan backward from there.

For most intimate weddings at a single venue in Nassau County or western Suffolk County, 8 hours is the honest answer. For larger celebrations across multiple locations, East End weddings with long drives built in, or couples who want the full arc of the night from getting ready through the last song, 12 hours gives you that story.

Frequently asked questions

Does Mystudionet Productions offer both 8-hour and 12-hour packages?

Yes. My packages start at $3,500 for 8 hours of cinematic coverage, with a premium option going up to 12 hours for larger or longer weddings. Every package includes professional color grading, licensed music, and delivery in 8 to 12 weeks. I take a limited number of weddings each season, so the sooner you reach out, the better chance your date is still open.

Can I upgrade from 8 hours to 12 hours after I book?

Most couples who upgrade do so after sitting down with their planner or coordinator and realizing the timeline runs longer than they first imagined. Reach out before your wedding date and I will let you know if the extended coverage is still available. It almost always is when there is enough notice.

What happens if my wedding runs over the booked hours?

I will not leave in the middle of your send-off. The smart move is to discuss an hourly extension rate at the time of booking so there are no surprises if the night goes long. Every couple I work with knows exactly what to expect before we ever get to their wedding day.

Do both 8-hour and 12-hour packages include the same editing and deliverables?

Yes. Both packages deliver the same cinematic highlight film, professional color grading, and licensed music. The 12-hour package gives me more footage to draw from, which can add texture and a fuller arc to the final film, but the craft and care going into the edit is the same either way.
